# Viewpoints and the Map

The Map of both layers, its links and its highlight. How the editor draws and keeps them is in `src/main/editor/presentation/CLAUDE.md`.

## The Map of the technology layer

It draws the applications and the relationships, and both can be hidden or shown. What is drawn is settled once, in the kernel's `map` (contract 5, additive keys): `applications` and `relationships` are optional booleans of the input (absent or null means drawn), and `app_elements` is the application layer the map was not given before. A technology map that gives no `app_elements`, and the Map of the application layer whatever it is given, are drawn exactly as before.

- **The applications** are the `applications` of the nodes of the layer, a box each inside its node. A box is drawn only when its node is open (`opened`, or it encloses a pinned element) and visible, and, when the map is pinned, only inside what it is pinned on. Opening a box shows its children one level down, so the applications of a node show only when the node itself is open. Their ids are the ones the deep viewpoint gives (`<last segment of the node>_<application>`, or the whole path of the node when that is ambiguous or is the reference of an element), settled over the whole layer so that opening or hiding a box renames none. A node that hosts applications is `expandable` while it is closed; an application box has nothing to open. A pin or a hidden element names technology elements only: an application is not pinned, hidden or opened on its own (hide its node, or turn *Applications* off).
- **The relationships** are the ones the elements of the layer declare, and the dependencies between environments that the deployed applications give, the ones the overview, the detailed and the deep viewpoints draw (`derived_dependencies`): an application whose environment does not host the destination, found in an environment or a node that environment `uses`. `uses` stays a scope, never a link of its own; the dependencies inside one environment stay out, as they are "secondary" in those viewpoints. A dependency is a link between the boxes of the two applications when both are drawn, else between the boxes of the nodes that host them, grouped under the first closed box as every relationship of the map is.
- A dependency uses the relationships of the deployed element and of everything inside it (`relationships_of_deployed` in the kernel): a relationship of `shop.app.payments` is a dependency of the node that hosts `shop.app`, in the three viewpoints and the Map alike; the relationship of the link says who declares it; one that ends inside the deployed application itself is internal and gives nothing. The other end counts too: a relationship whose destination is inside a deployed application is a dependency towards the node that hosts it (`deployed_application_of`, the nearest deployed application; the link is drawn between the boxes of the deployed applications and the relationship of the link says what it ends on); the destination must still be inside a deployed application, and one that ends inside the deployed application it starts from stays internal. The secondary links inside one environment, drawn for a flow only, read the relationships of everything inside the deployed element on both ends as the primary links do. Tests: `DestinationDependenciesTest`, `destination-dependencies.test.js`, the kernel's `the_secondary_links_read_what_is_inside_the_deployed_element_on_both_ends`; the fixture `viewpoint-kernel/workspace` holds the golden views of its four faceted folders.
- **The toggles**: the properties of a Map view are `applications` and `relationships`, booleans, drawn when absent. The editor writes `false` only, after the lists and `facet` and `hide-unlinked` (`VIEW_PROPERTY_ORDER`, `canonicalViewProperties`); a value that is not the boolean `false` is no choice, in both tools; they mean nothing in a Map of the application layer and `canonicalViewDefinition` takes them out of anything else. They are read by `KernelViewpointFactory.mapParts` and by the page (`mapPartsOf(view)`), who both only pass them to the kernel.
- **An application box** is an element of the other layer: its `data-ref` is the application and its `data-layer` is `application` (on the Map only; `renderDiagramSvg`), and a click selects it in the application layer. The panel does not say "the map draws the technology layer" for an application that is deployed on some node (`applicationIsDeployed`, `mapElementIsOnMap(reference, layer)`, `scene.drawnApps`), and `mapBoxesIfOpened` counts the applications of the nodes it would open.
- A saved view of the technology Map that says neither key draws both. `src/test/resources/technology-map/expected-boxes.json` is the contract of the boxes and links both tools draw on the saved maps of `domain_technology_map` and of the layered Brightcart, with the variants that leave the two parts out. On the layered Brightcart the `environments_map` and `cloud_map` of every plateau, comparison and facet folder draw the link `prod.dc --> partners` (or `prod.cloud.k8s --> partners`) and, when pinned, the `partners` box; `domain_technology_map` deploys no application.

## The `links` of a Map

- A saved Map view of the application layer may say which relationships it draws: `properties.links`, a list of mappings `{source, destination, label}` (`label` optional; `MapLinks`, `KernelViewpointFactory.mapLinks`). The kernel's `map` reads the key `links` of its input (additive, contract 5 unchanged).
- The match is exact on the source, the destination and the label (`RelationshipLabel.same`; no label is not a wildcard). Relationships of one triple that differ by qualifiers are drawn together. The boxes are the ends of the matched relationships, the pinned elements and their ancestors; `hidden` and `pinned` filter the relationships as before. `links` absent, `null` or `[]` is no selection.
- An entry of another shape is one `error:` line (``the view v: `links[n]` is a mapping with `source`, `destination` and optionally `label`, and ... is not one``); an entry that names no relationship another (``the view v: `links[n]` names a relationship from `a` to `b` with the label `x`, which does not exist``, or `with no label`); an unknown end is `dangling reference`; `links` on a map of the technology layer is refused. All exit 1.
- In a workspace with plateaus or a facet the adapter drops an entry the plateau lacks, and `ViewInPlateau` warns (``the view `v` is drawn without the link `a -> b`, which the plateau `p` does not have``) or skips the view (``skip `v`: none of its links is in the plateau `p` ``), so a typo is only a warning there. The load check of `links` in a layered workspace is not built. The cases are `src/test/javascript/viewpoint-kernel/map-links-cases.json`; manual `[[map-links]]`.

## The `highlight` of a Map

- A Map view of the application layer, and a story step, may name relationships to draw heavy: `properties.highlight` on a view and the key `highlight` on a step (`Story.Step#highlight`), a list of the same `{source, destination, label}` entries as `links`. `MapLinks` reads both keys (shape, exact match, `existsInUnion` in a comparison; sentences with `highlight[n]`); the cases are the `highlight` section of `map-links-cases.json`.
- The kernel's `map` reads the optional input `highlight` and marks `highlighted: true` on an output link that stands for a highlighted relationship (a grouped link when any of its relationships is; absent otherwise; marked after `merge_links`; `only_changes` drops the mark with the link; additive, contract 5 unchanged). `KernelViewpointFactory` forwards it for `viewpoint: map` only (other viewpoints ignore it; the technology layer refuses it), and `Link#highlighted` carries it.
- `rendering/plantuml/HighlightMarks`, called by `LinkRenderer`: a view is highlighting when one link is. A highlighted link is `thickness=5` (`#D55E00`, or the arrow of its state at that weight), with the glyph U+25B6 and a bold label. A link with no state and not secondary is calm (`-[#A6A6A6]->`, label in grey); a link with a state that is not highlighted, and a secondary link, are drawn as before. A view with no highlighted link is byte for byte as before.
- On a step, `GenerateStoriesCommand.viewOf` replaces the `highlight` of the view it draws (a copy of the saved view, which is untouched; `[]` switches it off; the step wins over its own `properties.highlight`).
- Plateau rule: a missing entry is an `error:` without plateau or facet. In a workspace of plateaus or facets `ViewInPlateau` warns (``... is drawn without the highlight `a -> b`, which the plateau `p` does not have``) and the view is still drawn, never skipped for it (unlike `links`).
- The manual says it in `[[map-highlight]]` and `[[stories-generate]]`, the guide in its table of view properties, its stories paragraph and its bullet on a story of a flow.
