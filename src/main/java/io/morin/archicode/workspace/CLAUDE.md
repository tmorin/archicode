# Workspace, plateaus and the discovery of manifests

Plateaus as layers, the manifests that are read, the layered load, and plateau fold. Moved from the root `CLAUDE.md` with its wording unchanged; the root file says where each area lives.

## Where the manifests are

Where the manifests are: an entry of `settings.manifests.paths` that ends in `/**` is read recursively, any other entry reads the files directly in its folder. `manifest/ManifestDiscovery` (the command line) and `readManifestFolder` in `standalone.html` (the editor) apply one rule, run on one list of cases, `src/test/javascript/viewpoint-kernel/discovery-cases.json`, by `ManifestDiscoveryTest` and `discovery.test.js`: files sorted by their path from the folder of `.archicode.yaml`, compared by Unicode code point; below a `/**` path, no hidden file or folder, no link to a folder (`link` in the list of `editor serve`), and no folder of the generated views (`views.path` at its depth, and the folders beside it that begin with its name and a dash), and no folder that holds both files a comparison writes (`gap.json` and `gap.md`, whichever `--output` and whichever command: `ManifestDiscovery.holdsComparison`, `holdsComparisonFiles` in the page; a lone `gap.json` is read, since it may be a manifest); `**` anywhere else in an entry is a one-line error (`ManifestDiscovery.pathProblems`, `manifestsPathProblems` in the page). A change to the rule is a change to the cases file. A manifest is YAML, JSON or TOML on the command line (`ManifestParser` reads the file with the mapper of its extension, then its `content` again as JSON text with the JSON mapper, so a TOML manifest gives the same model as its YAML twin: `TomlManifestTest`, case D36); the editor reads YAML only and lists the `.toml` and `.json` files it finds as files it does not read. The kinds of a manifest are the seven elements, `view`, `story` and `plateau` (`ManifestKind`, `ARCHICODE_KIND_MAP`, `READ_SCHEMA.manifestKinds`): `group` is no kind either, in both tools (the notion of group is removed, with `ApplicationGroup`, `ContainerGroup`, `SolutionGroup`, `SystemGroup`, `Item.Kind.GROUP`, the `group` of the creation form, of *Move* and of the nesting level; a nested element of `kind: group` is ``unknown kind `group`, a group is no kind any more: write its children in the `elements` of its parent (known: ...) at `elements[0]` `` and a header of `archicode.morin.io/group` (or the old `container-group`, `solution-group`, `system-group`) is `unrecognized header.kind` with the same words before the list, `ArchiCodeException.GROUP_IS_GONE` and `GROUP_IS_GONE` in the page; N89 and N90 of `negative-cases.json`; the kernel knows none, and the children of a former group go in the `elements` of its parent; N56 to N58 pin the three old header forms).

## The tree of plateaus

- Plateaus are layers (next paragraph).
- `settings.plateaus`, `plateau add` and `--baseline` no longer exist.
- And a workspace file that has the key fails on every command with the one `error:` line of `WorkspaceFactory.REMOVED_KEY`, pinned by `RemovedPlateausKeyTest`.
- `Settings` has no `plateaus` property and `READ_SCHEMA` no entry for it, so the sentence for an unknown key of `settings` does not list it.
- `settings.current` and `--plateau` choose the plateau.
- `--plateau` is looked at before `settings.current`, so it gets past a `current` that names a plateau that is gone.
- Layered plateaus: a manifest of an element or a plateau says `plateau: <id>` in its header (none: `baseline`, the implicit root; a view and a story belong to no plateau, see below), and an optional manifest of kind `archicode.morin.io/plateau` (`resource/plateau/Plateau`: `id`, `name`, `horizon`, `override` — one plateau, absent means `baseline` — and `patches`, read by `Patch` and carried as data for the kernel's `apply_patches`) qualifies it.
- `ManifestParser.parse` builds a `resource/plateau/PlateauTree` from them (ids in `[a-z0-9_-]`, declared once, a known `override`, no cycle: one `error:` line each; a plateau that only headers name, or two ids one letter apart when at least one of the two only has headers to name it (the typo; two plateaus with a manifest each, `p1` and `p2`, give none; also two `SET` of one field of one target in one plateau, the last wins, and a horizon earlier than the parent's, both read as periods: a warning through `Warnings`), and `PlateauTree.redeclared` refuses a reference declared in a plateau and again in its ancestor (two plateaus that do not stack may each declare it).
- A tree that forks needs `--plateau`, as for every command that loads a plateau.
- `--plateau` names the plateau (default `settings.current`, else the last one when the tree is one line), `--against` the plateau to compare with (default the one it overrides; the root has none to default to), `plateau list` lists the tree, `views-<plateau>` and `views-<plateau>-vs-<against>` are the folders,
- `plateau-model-cases.json` is read by `PlateauModelTest` and `plateau-model.test.js`.

## Views and stories, which belong to no plateau

- **Views and stories belong to no plateau**: only elements, patches and plateaus are layers. `Parsed.placedViews`/`placedStories` hold them with their file and no plateau; a view (a story) is declared once in the whole workspace (the existing `declared twice` error, even across plateaus that do not stack); `plateau:` in their header is accepted and means nothing: no warning, no error, no plateau made of it (nothing but Brightcart consumes a plateau on a story or a view; `ManifestParser.parse` skips the key for these two kinds, `buildPlateauTree` and `isPlateauFreeManifest` in the page; M01, M84 to M87 of `plateau-model-cases.json`, N79 of `negative-cases.json`, which now say "no warning").
- The check that remains is plateau-free and told once, after the layers (`LayerLoader.entryProblems`, `entryProblemsOf` in the page; K04, K05, K32 to K36 and N80): every element a view or a story step names must be *declared* by a manifest or `.archicode.yaml` in some plateau (`Parsed.declared`), else ``the view `v` (`file`) names `x`, which exists in no plateau``; patches are not resolved for it, since a patch never creates an element. Only a workspace that has plateaus is checked.
- A name that another plateau has and the plateau drawn lacks is no mistake of the view: the command line leaves it out of a map (`KernelViewpointFactory.mapLists`) with a warning, and skips, with a warning, a map none of whose pinned elements is there and a view whose element is not there (`GenerateViewsCommand.canBeDrawnInPlateau`, which asks the plateau a facet was applied to; N81, N82), and the page says the view cannot be drawn in this plateau (`viewProblem`, `viewLacksInPlateau`).
- **A step of a story may be told at a plateau and compared with another one** (the keys are `at` and `against`, not the header's `plateau:`: `Story.Step#at`, `#against`; none: the plateau drawn, no comparison; `against` with no `at` is told at the plateau drawn; an empty text names nothing): each must be a plateau of the workspace and the two cannot be one plateau, else one `error:` line with the story, the file and the number of the step, told with the plateau-free check, step by step and before the names the step carries (`LayerLoader.stepPlateauProblems`, `stepPlateauProblems` in the page; K37 to K40, N83 to N85), also for a workspace that has no plateau (`WorkspaceFactory.requireNoPlateauInSteps`, `StepPlateausTest`: the shared cases are all layered).
- The command line checks them at the load, and draws them with a command of its own, `archicode stories generate [--story <id>]...` (`views generate` does not draw stories; `cli/GenerateStoriesCommand`, `StoriesGroup`, `ViewInPlateau` shared with `views generate`; `GenerateStoriesTest`, `StoriesGenerateIT`):
- Each step is drawn like a view of the plateau it is told at (`at`, else `--plateau`/`settings.current`; a plateau is loaded once, a comparison made once) and, when it says `against`, like the views of a comparison (the same `compare` and union as `views generate`, the facets of `settings.facets` are drawn as `views generate` draws them, the global views in the step folder unless `global-enabled` is false (with no facet either the command says once that nothing is drawn), one folder per facet beside it named with the template of the facets applied to the step folder, `<NN>-<view>-<facet>`, made on the union for a compared step, and a facet that removes the element the step names is the warning ``skip <story>/<n> in the facet `f`: it names `x`, which the facet removes`` and that folder only is skipped; a step that says `against` also gets `gap.md` and `gap.json` in its own step folder, the table `views generate` writes for the pair, `GapFiles`, `GapReport.render`, none in a facet folder)
- The files go to `<views.path>-stories/<story>/<NN>-<view>/` (the views path without the plateau suffix; `NN` from 01; `<view>` is the view the step names, else the viewpoint of the view it carries, whose file is `step_NN.puml`), a map is drawn

## Comparing plateaus

- A comparison loads its two plateaus once each, parsing the manifests once for each, and `createUnion` is made from a copy of the target already loaded.
- And `compare` (two plateaus in, the union with a `change` on every element and relationship and a sorted table of changes out; `compute` takes that `change` back).
- An element of the more recent plateau may say `replaces: [ref, ...]` (references of the plateau it is compared with; never guessed, read by `compare` only, kernel contract 4): it is then `moved`, `split` or `merged` and the replaced ones are no longer `removed`.
- `compare` also lists candidates (same kind and a name that no other removed and no other new element of that kind has, a close description or a lone sibling, the last only when one of the two plateaus is the past of the other: the host tells `compare` with `on_one_chain`, required since kernel contract 5), never applied: the CLI prints them only for a plateau that already uses `replaces`, the editor proposes them for any comparison.
- The computed states are `new`, `changed`, `removed`, `moved`, `split`, `merged` and, for a box that only contains changes, `contains-changes`.
- Stereotypes added last to the box, with a default style that `styles.by-tags` overrides property by property, and given only to a box that has the state computed.
- The kernel says so with `contains_changes` on the item and on the elements of `compare`'s union too, with `contains_more_than_wording` beside it for the editor's wording toggle.
- And `StateTags.stateOf` and the page's `comparisonStateOfElement` read the key and derive nothing from the references.
- A hand-written state word that disagrees is only the author's stereotype, and the command says so on the error output, as it does for the warnings of `replaces`, see below.
- `archicode diff` and `views generate` write the table (`gap.md`, `gap.json`, with the identity and the candidates) and `views-<plateau>-vs-<against>`.
- `diff --fail-on <state>[,<state>...]` (the seven words of `KernelStates` and `any`, `cli/FailOn`; the option may be repeated) exits with 3 when the table holds a change of a listed state, 0 otherwise, and prints the table as it does without the option.
- With `--no-wording` a row that is wording only does not count.
- An unknown or empty state is one `error:` line, exit 1, before anything is loaded.
- `diff --width <columns>` wraps the text table (a cell at its spaces, a reference or a list without any after its dots, slashes and commas, then where the line ends).
- The columns share the width, the short ones keep theirs, and the counts and the sentences wrap too.
- `rendering/gap/TextWrap`, `cli/Width`.
- Not a whole number above zero is one `error:` line, exit 1, before anything is loaded.
- Without the option the text is byte for byte what `archicode-0.14.0` printed.
- `markdown`, `json` and the `gap.*` files of `views generate` are never wrapped, so the option is `diff`'s alone.
- `TextWrapTest`, `DiffWidthTest`, `PlateauComparisonIT`.
- `views generate --only-changes` passes the kernel's `only_changes` to the views of the comparison only (`views-<plateau>-vs-<against>` and its facets: the views of the plateau and the table are as they were) and is an `error:` line when nothing is compared.
- `PlateauComparer` leaves out of a layered target the `replaces` that the base writes the same way for the same element, decided from the two elements when the kernel's graph is built (`KernelGraph.of` takes the test) and not by editing the JSON afterwards (an inherited element keeps the `replaces` of the layer that declared it, which would otherwise warn once per element; whether an entry is news depends on the plateau it is compared with, so the load of one plateau cannot take it out: against the root the `replaces` of `inhouse` are kept);
- A comparison several layers apart composes the `replaces` of the layers in between, so that a move made in two steps is a move (in the kernel: the host hands over the plateaus, the kernel rewrites):
- `compare` takes an optional `chain` (additive to contract 5, the number does not move; rule 8 of `compare.rs`, "`chain`" in the kernel's README): the plateaus the target stands on, oldest first, from the base (between siblings: the last plateau they have in common, `PlateauTree.standsOn`) to the plateau the target overrides, each as `reference` and the `replaces` its layer wrote;
- With two plateaus or more the kernel reads an entry of the target that names an element of the plateau just above through the step that made it, down to the first plateau (a move then a move is a `moved` from the first plateau's element, a move then a split or a merge is a split or a merge, an element created in a layer in between and moved by the next is `new` and the entry is no mistake, one that a layer in between removed ends its chain; an entry the last step cannot explain is left as written, so the usual warning of rule 6 tells it, and `warnings` and the `replaces` of the union say the composed words).
- The command line builds `chain` in `PlateauComparer` from `Workspace.getSteps()` (`LayerLoader` keeps one `PlateauStep` for each plateau of the chain: the elements of both layers once the layer is applied),
- A comparison with the plateau a plateau overrides sends none and is as it was,
- The command line writes no `replaces` and has no such rule.
- `compose-replaces-cases.json` is read by `ComposeReplacesCasesTest` and `compose-replaces.test.js`; P4 of the Brightcart fixture is now verified by `archicode diff` and by the editor.
- The tables of `diff` do not depend on them, and a saved view is drawn by `views generate` only when it is named with `-i`, so the files of the plain run are the same.

## The kernel

- The kernel has six entry points.
- `compute` (views).
- `apply_facet`.
- `states` (the seven state words, said once).
- `quiet` (which elements of a layer have nothing to draw, for all of them in one call: the editor greys them in its tree, one call per layer however many elements; the command line has no use for it and `ViewpointKernel` does not expose it).
- `compute` removes both keys from its input, so a facet does not see them.
- The kernel says the seven words once with its `states` entry point, which `KernelStates` and the page read.

## Plateau fold

**`archicode plateau fold <id> [--dry-run]`**: folds a plateau into the one above it, or says in one `error:` line why it cannot, and `--dry-run` runs everything but the write. `workspace/Fold` holds the refusals and the plan in plain Java (no Picocli, no CDI), `FoldReport` prints it (present tense for the dry run, past with `(written)`), `PlateauFoldService` reads the facts through `WorkspaceFactory.facts`, loads the plateau above once, and drives the rest; `FoldRewrite` changes the manifests in memory (the header of those that name the folded plateau, the `override` and the patches of the plateau above, the `replaces` composed; each keeps its format, YAML, TOML or JSON, and loses its comments, quotes and layout), `FoldCheck` folds on a temporary copy and compares the model of each plateau that stays and the table of every pair before and after (only the PLATEAU and PATCH columns of the cascade may differ; the check runs in the dry run too, and nothing forces it), `FoldFiles` writes all its files or none (`FoldFiles.commit`: re-read the bytes, stage `<file>.archicode-fold`, move over the targets, delete the folded manifest last, put back from memory on a failure; `DiskFoldFiles` is the disk, a test double fails each step in `FoldFilesTest`; the guarantee is for a failed write, not a killed process), `FoldSnapshots` holds the snapshots, `cli/FoldCommand` is the command. The deleted manifest's `name` and `horizon` are not kept and the report says so. The sentences are the cases of `src/test/javascript/viewpoint-kernel/fold-cases.json` (F01 to F08, F20 to F27, with `expect.files`), read by `PlateauFoldServiceTest` and `PlateauFoldCommandTest` (`FoldTest`, `FoldRewriteTest`, `FoldCheckTest` hold their own data) and by `fold-workspace.test.js`, which opens the folded workspaces in the editor; `ManifestRoundTripCorpusTest` holds the round trip of the manifests the repository ships; the manual says it in `[[plateau-fold]]`, `[[fold-by-hand]]` and `[[plateau-limits]]`.
