## Layout and answers

The reading is a **matrix**: rows are elements, columns are the plateaus of one path of the tree, plus one column that compares the first plateau with the last. The mock-up is `mockup-0206-n-plateaus.html` (beside this file; open it in a browser; the pins Q1 to Q5 on the page mark the place of each answer below; it shows 14 rows of the 88, from the real output of `archicode diff`, and switches between the three paths and the two readings). Every number below is a row of the record in Findings (T2), with its command.

**Q1. Rows and columns.** *Finding:* the tools read two plateaus at a time (`diff --plateau X --against Y`; *Compare with* on the band; `gap.json`); the page Plateaus shows, for each plateau, the counts against the plateau it overrides (`timelineChangesHtml`), never one element across plateaus. A row by reference is wrong as soon as an element is renamed or moved: `commerce.search` exists only in `inhouse`, `discovery.search` only in `marketplace`, and they are one element. *Proposal:* a **row is one lineage**: an element followed through the `replaces` of each pair, named by its reference in the last plateau where it exists; a **column is one plateau**, in tree order; the first column says only present or not; each other column says the state of the row in that plateau compared with the plateau on its left (*Step by step*, the default) or with `baseline` (*Against baseline*, one switch); a split is drawn on two rows that name the same origin and a merge on one row with two origins in the first cell. Rows are grouped by layer, in the order of the Elements page, and the rows with no change in any column are folded (the same switch as *Changes only*). *Brightcart, path `baseline`, `inhouse`, `marketplace`:* 4 columns (3 plateaus and the span), 88 rows (82 elements of `marketplace` and 6 removed), 61 with a change and 27 without; by reference there are 103 distinct references, so 15 references are the old names of a row. Relationships are not rows: 80 relationship identities in the three plateaus and 45 changed in the span (35 new, 9 removed, 1 changed); they are the nature `relationships` of the cell of their source element, and the layout leaves a collapsed second block for them to the build wave (not drawn in the mock-up). *Place in the mock-up:* the table under "The matrix" (pin Q1) and its row labels.

**Q2. Cumulative change.** *Finding:* the sum of the cells is not the cumulative change: the pairs say 7 + 5 = 12 moved and 5 + 12 = 17 changed, one comparison of `baseline` with `marketplace` says 11 moved and 3 changed (an element that is moved and then changed twice is one move). By reference, folding the two pairs with a simple dominance rule (new, removed, moved, split and merged win over changed) gives the state of the one comparison on 61 of 63 rows; the two rows it gets wrong are `commerce.search` and `shop.web`, the two names that are cells of a row, so with lineage rows it agrees on all 61. *Proposal:* the **Span column is the state of ONE `compare` of the first plateau with the last, with `chain`** (rule 8 of `compare.rs`, wave 017), never a fold of the cells; the kernel already composes `replaces` and keeps the natures of the whole way (`discovery.search`: kind, name, qualifiers). The dominance rule is how a reader sees the agreement, not code to write twice. A row that is new in a middle plateau and removed in a later one (a transient) has an empty Span and its two cells are the only trace; no row of the Brightcart is one, so this case is a proposal that no fixture proves, and the build wave adds a case for it. Cost: one extra `compare` per matrix. *Place:* the highlighted column "Span" (pin Q2) and the rows `commerce.cart` and `shop.app` (moved then changed, changed then removed).

**Q3. A `replaces` move across more than two plateaus.** *Finding:* `discovery.search` is declared in `marketplace/application/discovery.yaml` with `replaces: [commerce.search]`, `commerce.search` in `inhouse/application/commerce.search.yaml` with `replaces: [shop.app.search]`, and `marketplace/plateau.yaml` line 28 removes `commerce.search`. Pair `inhouse` against `baseline`: `commerce.search` moved from `shop.app.search` (kind, name, qualifiers, relationships); pair `marketplace` against `inhouse`: `discovery.search` moved from `commerce.search` (no nature); one comparison of `marketplace` against `baseline`: `discovery.search` moved from `shop.app.search`, with the before and after of the whole way (component to container, `Search` to `Search service`, qualifier `OpenSearch`). The story step 6 of `fork_tour` ("the search moved twice") already draws it. Other cases in the same files: `shop.web` is changed in `inhouse` and moved to `storefront.web` in `marketplace`; `shop.app.orders` is split into `orders.history` and `orders.placement` in `inhouse` and both are changed in `marketplace`; `shop.app.customers` and `shop.app.loyalty` are merged into `accounts.profiles`. *Proposal:* the row is `discovery.search`; the cell of `inhouse` says `moved`, "from `shop.app.search`" and the name it had there (`commerce.search`, found by following the `replaces` of the pair backwards); the cell of `marketplace` says `moved` from `commerce.search`; the Span says `moved` from `shop.app.search` with the natures; the detail under the table lists the name in each plateau and the before and after of the Span. Nothing is asked of the kernel. *Place:* the first row (pin Q3) and the panel under the table, which opens on it; `storefront.web`, `orders.*` and `accounts.profiles` are the other Q3 rows.

**Q4. A tree that forks.** *Finding:* `plateau list` gives `baseline`, `inhouse` and `saas` (both on the root, no `override`), `marketplace` over `inhouse`. A comparison of siblings works (`inhouse` against `saas`: 27 new, 4 changed, 3 removed, 2 split, 1 merged) but it is read as two alternatives, not a history: it prints 7 warnings that a `replaces` names an element the base (`saas`) does not have (`marketplace` against `saas` prints 7 too), and the `on_one_chain` flag of the kernel is false. *Proposal:* the tree is drawn above the matrix and is the control: choosing a plateau chooses its path from the root; the Brightcart has two paths, `baseline`, `inhouse`, `marketplace` (3 columns and the Span) and `baseline`, `saas` (one step, so Span is the step itself and is not drawn twice). A third choice, *All four, against baseline*, puts every plateau of the tree in its own column compared with the root (the three comparisons with `baseline`: `inhouse` 17 new, 5 changed, 1 removed; `marketplace` 38, 3, 6; `saas` 3, 5, 11); it is the one reading in which the two branches are side by side, and it never compares two siblings. No cell of the matrix is a sibling comparison; the one in the story is a deliberate step. The tree's own question, which plateau a reader wants on the right, stays one choice on the tree, and is in the address (see Q5). *Place:* the tree panel (pin Q4), the three controls, the saas column of the *All four* reading and the row `commerce-saas` (pin Q4).
