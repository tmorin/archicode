# Run 0100: Stories and views untied

Wave 015 (`editor-reshape-and-loose-ends`), item W-12 (phase P1). Focus: a story and a view exist whatever plateau is drawn; only elements, patches and plateaus are layers (#100, #86).

Document profile: `single-document`, as runs 0089 to 0099. The design is settled by the author's decisions of 2026-10-18 on #100 (a to e), which the brief repeats, and what is left to decide is a handful of rules, each of which fits in a line below and is pinned by a shared case; a prd/tdd/pln would only copy the issue and this list. The review passes (a read of this document against the code, the open questions, the C scan, a read-only review of the diff by a sub-agent) are answered below.

## Scope

In: the reader of the command line (`ManifestParser`, `PlateauTree.redeclared`, `LayerLoader`, `WorkspaceFactory`), the way the command line draws a saved view in a plateau that lacks an element it names (`GenerateViewsCommand`, `KernelViewpointFactory`), the editor (`standalone.html`: the model of the manifests, the checks, the pages Stories and Files, the writers), the shared cases (`negative-cases.json`, `plateau-model-cases.json`, `layer-checks-cases.json`) and the tests that taught A8, `CLAUDE.md` (the sentences that describe A8), `reflect-config.json` (two nested classes of `LayerLoader`).

## Decisions

- **D1. Only elements, patches and plateaus are layers.** A view and a story are read as before (file, header, content) and belong to no plateau: `Parsed.ofPlateau` returns elements only, `Placed` has no plateau, the views and stories are the workspace's.
- **D2. `plateau:` on a story or a view is a warning, the key is ignored.** One line, the same in both tools (`ManifestParser.ignoredPlateauWarning`, `ignoredPlateauWarning` in the page): ``warning: `<file>` is a <view|story> and says `plateau: <id>` in its header, which is ignored: a <view|story> exists whatever plateau is drawn``. It names no plateau (a plateau that only a story names is no plateau), does not make a workspace layered, and is told after the warnings of the tree. Cases: M01, M84 to M86 of `plateau-model-cases.json` (both tools), N79 of `negative-cases.json` (command line `diff`, page `treeWarnings`). The page tells it also for a workspace with no plateau (`renderPlateauTreeHtml`).
- **D3. One namespace for views and one for stories.** A view (a story) declared twice anywhere, whatever the plateaus, is the existing error ``the view `x` is declared twice...`` (M42, M86). The rule "declared in a plateau and again in its ancestor" is gone for them (`PlateauTree.redeclared` is about references only). Two plateaus that do not stack can no longer each declare the same view: it is one view.
- **D4. The plateau-free check (decision b).** In a workspace with plateaus, every element a view (`properties.element`, `pinned`, `opened`, `hidden`) or a story step that carries its own recipe names must exist in **at least one plateau**; one that exists in none is an error, ``the view `v` (`file`) names `x`, which exists in no plateau`` (``the story `s` ...``), told once, after the layers (a mistake of a layer comes first), whichever plateau is asked for. The views and stories that `workspace.yaml` still keeps are checked too, with `workspace.yaml` for the file, after those of the manifests, unless a manifest has the id (it shadows them). "Exists in at least one plateau" is decided from what is *declared*: a reference is declared by a manifest or an element nested in one (in any plateau), or by `workspace.yaml` (`Parsed.declared`; `declaredReferencesOf` in the page). Patches are not evaluated: they never create an element (`id`, `parent` and `elements` are refused by `apply_patches`), so the only elements declared that exist in no plateau are those a plateau declares and removes in its own layer, and the check lets that one case through. That approximation is chosen on purpose: it is the same few lines in both tools, it needs no resolution of a plateau that is not drawn (a broken sibling cannot make the load of another fail, and the elements of a layer share a model that is changed in place, so it cannot be resolved twice from one parent), and it costs nothing when every name is in the plateau drawn. Only a workspace that has plateaus is checked, as before (a workspace without plateaus is byte-identical, and its saved views are looked at when they are drawn). A step on a view that is not declared stays what it is: a warning of the command line, "does not exist any more" on the page.
- **D5. Where the check is told.** Command line: at the end of `LayerLoader.load`, one `error:` line per name. Page: not a mistake of a plateau, so not in `layerModelOf`: `entryProblemsOf` (kept with the layer cache), listed in its own section of the dialog of the mistakes, counted by the button of the band, and part of `layerDigest`, so that a delete that would leave a view naming an element that no plateau has is refused as it was (`Not written: the view ... names ..., which exists in no plateau`). Cases K04, K05, K32 to K36 (`layer-checks-cases.json`: `cli.error`, `editor.entryProblems`) and N80.
- **D6. A name that is in some plateau and not in the plateau drawn (#86, the finding of W-11).** One rule for both tools: **it is left out, and a view that cannot be drawn without it cannot be drawn in that plateau.** An entry of `pinned`, `opened` or `hidden` that the plateau lacks is dropped from the map (the page did it already, silently; the command line did `error: dangling reference: x`, and now does it with `warning: the view `m` is drawn without `x`, which the plateau `p` does not have`); a map whose `pinned` entries are all missing, and a view whose `element` is missing, cannot be drawn in that plateau: the page says it in the view (*The element “x” is not in the plateau “p”.*, the view is *broken*; `viewProblem`, `viewLacksInPlateau`), and `views generate -i` skips it with `warning: skip `v`: it names `x`, which the plateau `p` does not have` (it is not an error: asking for a view in every plateau of a loop is legitimate). The rule is for a workspace that has plateaus; elsewhere a name that is not there is a typo and stays an error. A custom facet is asked about the plateau it was made from, so that what the facet removes stays the facet's (silent in a map, an error for the element of a view, as it always was) and what the plateau lacks is skipped in every facet. `views generate` goes through the ids it was given in order (a `TreeSet`), so that the warnings of a run come in one order. Cases: N81 and N82 (command line: stderr and exit code, page: `viewProblems`).
- **D7. A new view or story is a file like a manifest with no `plateau:`**, in the first folder of `manifests.paths`, with no `plateau` in its header (`createManifestEntry`). Its writers, and those of its fields, its steps, its rename and its delete, no longer ask which plateau is drawn and are no longer refused when it cannot be drawn (they are not in `WRITE_ACTIONS`); *Move to manifests* is no longer refused outside baseline. A rename of an element rewrites every view and story that names it, whatever the plateau drawn (before: those of the plateaus the write reaches).
- **D8. The editor stops knowing the plateau of an entry.** `layerChainSet`, `allStories`, `allSavedViews`, the `all` parameter of `manifestEntries`/`entriesOf`, `writeScopeHasEntry`, `entryWriteRefusal`, `requireWritableEntry`, `writesRefused`, the note of the Stories page about the plateaus (`renderLayerEntriesNoticeHtml`), `LAYER_UNSUPPORTED_ACTIONS` and the clearing of the selection of a story or a view when a plateau is drawn are gone. `plateauOfManifest` and `filePlateauOf` return null for a manifest of kind view or story, so Files puts them in the group *No plateau*, with no change to the page (the group was written generic by W-10).

## Findings

- F1. For **W-13** (the optional plateau and comparison of a step): nothing here makes it harder. A step already carries its own recipe; `entryProblemsOf` and `LayerLoader.entryProblems` read `step.properties` and `step.layer` only, so two new keys `at` and `against` are free; the page draws a step in the plateau drawn (`stepView`, `stepProblem`, `viewProblem`), and the place to resolve a step against another plateau is where `viewProblem` asks `AppState.index`. The names the check reads must exist in at least one plateau, not in the one the step names, so W-13 may add the sentence for a step whose `at` plateau lacks a name (`viewLacksInPlateau` is the model). `Placed` and `Parsed` have room: `Story.steps` is read as it is.
- F2. For **W-18** (the manual and the tutorial), `src/doc/README.adoc` must say: a view and a story belong to no plateau (lines 221 and 251: the header sentence "a manifest of any kind (an element, a view, a story)" is for an element and a plateau; a `plateau:` on a view or a story is a warning and the key is ignored, the sentence of D2); line 1329 (*Views and stories cannot be patched*) and the limit #86 go: a view whose element a plateau lacks cannot be drawn in that plateau (D6, with the two warnings, and the page's *is not in the plateau*), and the check that remains is *exists in at least one plateau* (D4, its sentence, a workspace without plateaus is not checked); lines 1365 to 1375 and 1446 (the page Stories lists the same stories and views whatever plateau is drawn), 1503 (*Drawing a plateau*: Stories no longer follow the plateau), 1552 (*A view or a story another plateau declares* goes: they are edited and deleted from any plateau); a new story or view is written in the first folder of `manifests.paths` with no `plateau:`; *Move to manifests* works in every plateau; Files lists them in *No plateau*; and a story or a view declared twice is one error even across plateaus.
